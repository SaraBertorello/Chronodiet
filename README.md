# Chronodiet
This study is the first to investigate whether a chronotype-adapted diet is more effective than a conventional calorie-distributed diet in promoting weight loss and improving cardiometabolic risk factors and GM.

Type of samples: human stool
Sequencing: Illumina Novaseq 6000, 2x250

Title: "Effects of a chronotype-adapted diet on weight loss, cardiometabolic health and
gut microbiome: the CHRONODIET Study"

ABSTRACT
Introduction: Emerging evidence suggests that aligning dietary patterns with an individual’s
circadian rhythm, or chronotype, may optimize metabolic processes and gut microbiome (GM)
composition and function, but evidence from clinical trials is still limited.
Objectives: This study is the first to investigate whether a chronotype-adapted diet is more
effective than a conventional calorie-distributed diet in promoting weight loss and improving
cardiometabolic risk factors and GM.
Methods: In this 4-month parallel-group, randomized, two-arm, open-label, superiority trial, 142
adults with overweight/obesity were assigned to either a chronotype-adapted, low-calorie diet
with meal timing tailored to their metabolic peaks (morning vs. evening chronotype) or a standard
control low-calorie eating plan. The primary outcome was weight change. Secondary outcomes
were changes in body composition, biochemical markers, GM composition, and short-chain fatty
acids (SCFAs).
Results: A total of 117 participants (84%; mean age 49±12 years) completed the study (57
intervention, 60 control). 
Conclusions: A chronotype-adapted diet may be more effective than a standard low-calorie diet in
improving body composition, metabolic risk profile, and GM in people with overweight/obesity –
particularly among evening chronotypes, who may face greater challenges with conventional
dietary strategies.
